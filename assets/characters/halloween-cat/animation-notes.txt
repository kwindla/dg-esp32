Halloween Cat animation asset pack

Open index.html or preview.html for the interactive app. It automatically shows
a silent listening-and-talking demo. Listen to me measures live microphone audio
locally and uses its energy to drive eye attention and whisker movement. Hear the
cat speaks the typed line with the browser's speech voice; utterance start/end
events gate mouth motion, with word boundaries nudging an approximate rhythm.
This is not phoneme alignment or analysis of synthesized speech PCM. No Pipecat
conversation service is connected. Open the HTTPS ngrok URL for microphone use.
The microphone is stopped on exit, cancel, switching modes, or hiding the app.
No microphone recording or upload is performed by this app.
sprite-workbench.html retains the original manual layer controls.
halloween-cat-animated-preview.gif is a 412x412, 20 fps, 4-second demonstration.

halloween-cat-faceplate-412.png is the blank underlying face with the nose retained.
sprites/ contains 15 transparent feature patches. animation.json defines their
absolute top-left placement and shared bounds in the 412x412 display coordinate
system. PNG transparency is real alpha. The original static cat is preserved.
sprites.js defines editable pixel rectangles; rerun node build-sprites.cjs after
editing it to regenerate the PNGs and manifest. No package installation is needed.

Eyes: happy, open, half, closed, left, right.
Mouth: smile, closed, small, medium, wide, round.
Whiskers: rest, lift, drop. The features move independently of the hat and body.
Mouth revision 2 keeps the original cat-smile corners and nose stem while the
openings taper into rounded bowls. The O shape is a stepped circular opening;
closed speech frames retain the resting smile. Tongues have curved pixel edges.
The nose stays static. Feature geometry uses a 103x103 logical grid at 4x scale.

Firmware integration: restore the clean face plate underneath each changed layer
before compositing its next sprite. Queue expression changes onto the display
task. Gate mouth animation by bot speaking and select opening from smoothed
audio energy at speaker playout, accounting for the playback buffer. This is
speech-reactive motion, not phoneme-accurate lip synchronization. Use independent
blink and whisker timers. Keep large image data out of internal DMA staging.

Validation: browser rendering matched fresh full composites for all 108 combinations
of eyes, mouth, and whiskers, with no stale pixels. Automatic simulated speech
produced closed, small, medium, wide, and round mouth frames. The GIF was checked
for 412x412 dimensions, 80 frames, and 4000ms total duration. JavaScript syntax and
sprite export bounds passed. Firmware integration, LCD optical verification,
performance, and RAM budgets remain pending. Interactive app was checked through
the public HTTPS tunnel at desktop and 390px phone widths, with no horizontal
overflow. Generated audio through a MediaStream exercised microphone energy,
eye/whisker reactions, and stopped tracks. Permission denial and cancellation
of a pending microphone request fall back or stop cleanly. Simulated speech
start/end/stop events verified mouth motion and cleanup. The automated browser
had no real speech voices installed: its speech failure fallback was checked;
audible speech on a physical phone remains unverified.

Face plate edited with the built-in image_gen tool (faceplate-prompt.txt).
Feature patches are deterministic code-native pixel shapes. Exports use Node and
FFmpeg. The GIF contains simulated motion and is a review artifact, not a firmware
animation format.
